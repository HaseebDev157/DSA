Question 5 — Advantages and Disadvantages
Array (from Question 1)
Advantages:
Any book/index could be accessed instantly just by its position, since the array kept everything in one continuous block. It was simple to loop through and display every element in order.
Disadvantages:
The size had to be fixed upfront (MAX_SIZE), so it could run out of room. Inserting or deleting in the middle meant shifting every element after it, which got slower as the array filled up.
Stack (from Question 2)
Advantages:
Push and pop were both instant, since they only ever touched the top — no shifting needed at all. It naturally matched the "last plate placed is the first plate removed" behavior, so the logic was very close to how the real pile actually works.
Disadvantages:
Only the top plate was ever reachable — there was no way to check or grab a plate in the middle without popping everything above it first. If the stack filled up (top reached MAX_SIZE - 1), no more plates could be pushed until something was popped.
Queue (from Question 3)
Advantages:
Tokens were served in the exact order they arrived, which matched how a real bank counter should behave. Using a circular array meant slots freed up by dequeue could be reused, so the queue didn't "waste" space over time like a plain array would.
Disadvantages:
Tracking front and rear with wraparound (% MAX_SIZE) added extra logic compared to a simple array — it was easy to get the indices wrong. Like the others, capacity was still fixed, so the queue could become full even though, physically, there's no real limit on a bank line.
